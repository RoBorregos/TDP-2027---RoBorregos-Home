\subsection{Computer Vision}

\subsubsection{Person Perception.}
Faces are detected and encoded with InsightFace~\cite{arcface}, and their embeddings are matched against the gallery of known guests by cosine similarity. Body pose comes from YOLO11-pose, which estimates keypoints for all people in one pass. Gestures and postures are heuristically inferred from joint angles. For open-ended attributes such as clothing are inferred by querying Moondream2.

\subsubsection{Person Following.}
ByteTrack~\cite{bytetrack} on YOLOv8 tracks the followed person but loses identity after occlusions, as it ignores appearance. A Swin Transformer~\cite{swin} Re-ID model trained on Market-1501~\cite{zheng2015market} then recovers it: the target is embedded every 0.3\,s into a rolling 128-vector gallery, and the visible person whose cosine similarity to it exceeds 0.6 (values set empirically) is re-acquired. Re-ID runs asynchronously, never blocking tracking; distinguishing people with similar clothing is our next research focus.

\subsubsection{Object Recognition.}
A self-hosted web pipeline\footnote{\url{https://github.com/RoBorregos/home-pipelines}} automates synthetic dataset generation: from a 20-second phone video per object, GroundingDINO~\cite{grounding_dino} detects the object by class name, SAM3~\cite{sam3} segments it, and the crops are composited onto arena backgrounds to train YOLO11 on two NVIDIA A6000 GPUs. At RoboCup 2026, one team member recorded, trained and deployed a 30-class detector in 5 hours, reaching 97.76\% mAP@0.5:0.95 on validation. When new objects are announced shortly before a test, retraining is impractical. We therefore use a training-free alternative: 20 photographs are collected for each class and stored as a gallery of visual embeddings. During inference, YOLOE~\cite{yoloe} generates class-agnostic object proposals, while DINOv2~\cite{dinov2} extracts an embedding for each proposal and compares it with the corresponding class gallery. This approach achieved 92.2\% proposal recall and 80.6\% gated recall@1. Although these results are lower than those of the supervised detector, the method can be prepared within minutes and does not require model retraining, making it suitable for objects introduced immediately before a test.
