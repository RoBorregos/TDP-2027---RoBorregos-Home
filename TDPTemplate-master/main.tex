\begin{abstract}
This paper introduces FRIDA (Friendly Robotic Interactive Domestic Assistant), a General Purpose Service Robot developed for RoboCup@Home 2027. The system employs an architecture that integrates high-level reasoning with robust low-level actuation. The five main contributions are: (1) A clutter-aware placing strategy that optimizes target poses via spatial suitability heatmaps (95\% success rate in object-placement tasks); (2) A multi-stage interpretation pipeline employing schema-aligned parsing to bridge natural language and physical execution; (3) A low-cost, open-source gripper with fractal structure soft fingertips designed for adaptive grasping; (4) An open-source omnidirectional mecanum base with on-board kinematics and odometry fusion, providing full holonomic mobility; and (5) A multi-command scheduler that merges consecutive GPSR commands into a single plan minimizing Nav2 path length (39\% less driving in simulation). To support the community, the architecture, core modules and ROS 2 workspaces are open-sourced and available at: https://github.com/RoBorregos/home2.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
RoBorregos is the undergraduate robotics team of Tecnológico de Monterrey, Mexico, competing in the @Home OPL since 2023 (9th at RoboCup 2026). Its robot, FRIDA (Friendly Robotic Interactive Domestic Assistant), is a domestic service robot with one arm and an omnidirectional base; a photo and its hardware are given in the annex (Fig.~\ref{fig:frida}). This paper describes the current system, with emphasis on the work done since our 2026 team description paper. The team, documentation and development logs are available on our website.\footnote{\url{https://athome.roborregos.com/}}

\section{Research focus and interests}
The FRIDA project philosophy is focused on bridging the gap between semantic reasoning and reliable actuation in unstructured domestic environments. Driven by this goal, our research for the 2027 cycle targets specific bottlenecks in service robotics to provide reproducible contributions to the community. This year, we report significant progress in:
\begin{enumerate}

\item Context-Aware Manipulation: Fused environmental heatmaps with task constraints to generate optimal object-placing poses, ensuring collision-free trajectories in high-density clutter.

\item Reliable Neuro-Symbolic Grounding: Developed a synthetic dataset pipeline to fine-tune LLMs for reliable instruction grounding, a contribution recently published in MICAI \cite{10.1007/978-3-032-09037-9_24}.

\item Segmentation Dataset Pipeline: Upgraded semi-automatic data generation from bounding boxes to instance segmentation masks for cleaner annotations and more robust training.


\item Holonomic Mobility: Replaced the differential-drive Dashgo B1 with a custom open-source mecanum base that runs its kinematics and EKF odometry.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Scientific contributions and current work}

This section details development highlights and achievements per area.

\subsection{Object Manipulation}

\subsubsection{Motion Planning.}
RRTConnect~\cite{kuffner2000rrt} plans collision-free trajectories, keeping the end-effector upright while carrying an object to pour. To reduce collision-checking time, the planning scene uses simple primitives: RANSAC~\cite{martinez2022ransac} segments the table as an oriented box, and the target object is approximated by spheres fitted to its voxel-downsampled point cloud. The rest of the scene comes from an OctoMap built from the depth camera.

\subsubsection{Picking.}
Target objects are isolated by fusing 2D detections with depth data, and their type selects the grasp strategy. Flat items (e.g., cutlery) are grasped top-down at the handle end with a force-guarded descent; containers (baskets, bowls, cups) by straddling the rim point nearest the robot; clothes at their highest point. Other objects are solids: RANSAC [3] fits a sphere and an upright cylinder, a rectangle fits a box, and votes the shape. Candidate grasps on the fitted shape that collide with the table, a neighbour or the gripper, or exceed the gripper opening, are discarded; the five best-ranked are executed in turn: the motion planner moves to a pre-grasp pose and a straight Cartesian approach closes in. Grasp Pose Detection (GPD) [4] handles shelf and pouring grasps and is the fallback.

\subsubsection{Placing \& Pouring.}
Placing scores the whole table with Gaussian kernels that reward free space and penalize nearby objects and edges, multiplied by a directional map for spatial requests (e.g., ``in front of X'', Fig.~\ref{fig:manip}a); only front, back, left, right and ``close to'' are supported. For pouring, PCL~\cite{rusu20113d} estimates the container's rim height, and the object is tilted 15\,cm above it at a fixed angle, without feedback on the poured amount.

\subsubsection{Experimental Validation.} %ME ESTAN BLOQUEANDO 
Each object was picked 10 times on the real robot, with an operator resetting the scene and confirming that the object was retained (Table~\ref{tab:manipulation_results}). The force-guarded strategy addresses the main failure case of our previous GPD-only pipeline, thin cutlery, which GPD could not grasp reliably. Placing succeeded in 19 of 20 trials, including spatial requests; the single failure came from releasing the object too high. Smooth cylindrical objects and pouring remain to be evaluated systematically.

\subsubsection{Custom Gripper.}
A low-cost, open-source\footnote{\url{https://grabcad.com/library/robotic-gripper-34}} parallel gripper, driven by a rack-and-pinion and a high-torque servo, holds objects up to 1\,kg. Its fingers combine fractal-structured silicone covers~\cite{cuan} with soft inner materials that deform to complex geometries (Fig.~\ref{fig:manip}b).

\subsection{Computer Vision}

\subsubsection{Person Perception.}
Faces are detected and encoded with InsightFace, and their embeddings are matched against the gallery of known guests by cosine similarity. Body pose comes from YOLO11-pose~\cite{yolo11}, which estimates keypoints for all people in one pass. Gestures and postures are inferred from joint angles; arms that are occluded or outside the frame are reported as unknown. For open-ended attributes such as clothing, Moondream2~\cite{moondream} is queried on person crops.

\subsubsection{Person Following.}
YOLOv8 with ByteTrack~\cite{bytetrack} tracks the followed person frame by frame. Because ByteTrack uses no appearance information, the identity is lost after occlusions, so a Swin Transformer Re-ID model trained on Market-1501~\cite{joint_reid} recovers it. While the target is visible, an embedding is stored every 0.3,s in a rolling gallery of up to 128 vectors; when the track is lost, visible people are matched against this gallery by cosine similarity, and the best match above 0.6 is re-acquired as the target. Re-ID runs asynchronously so it never blocks the tracking loop. People with similar clothing remain the main failure case.

\subsubsection{Object Recognition.}
A self-hosted web pipeline\footnote{\url{https://github.com/RoBorregos/home-pipelines}} replaces manual labeling: from a 20-second phone video per object, GroundingDINO~\cite{grounding_dino} localizes the object by class name, SAM3~\cite{sam3} refines its mask, and the crops are composited onto real backgrounds to train YOLO on two NVIDIA A6000 GPUs. At RoboCup 2026, we recorded, trained and deployed a 30-class detector in 5 hours with a single operator, reaching 97.76\% mAP@0.5:0.95 on validation. For objects announced shortly before a test, a path without retraining uses 20 photos per class to embed and store; in runtime, YOLOE~\cite{yoloe} proposes class-agnostic boxes and DINOv2~\cite{dinov2} embeddings are matched against the per-class gallery, reaching 92.2\% proposal recall and 80.6\% gated recall@1, lower than the supervised detector but available within minutes.

\subsection{Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)}

\subsubsection{Speech.} The main communication interface with users is through audio. Speech-to-Text (STT) uses an adaptation of the WhisperLive~\cite{whisperlive} implementation with a faster-whisper backend, deploying \texttt{distil-large-v3}. The pipeline incorporates phrase-aware hotword handling and configurable language and transcription tasks. Speaker detection is complemented by a MediaPipe-based visual talking detection system, while DeepFilterNet3 provides noise cancellation. Text-to-Speech (TTS) uses the open-weight Kokoro-82M model~\cite{hexgrad_2025}, with playback timeout handling and support for headless operation. These services run in containers outside ROS and communicate through gRPC, preserving the microservice architecture.

\subsubsection{Display.} A secondary user interface is displayed on a screen in the robot's base. It provides insights into FRIDA's processes, allowing users to better understand how and when to interact with it, such as prompts to speak, live STT transcriptions, TTS dialog, detected keywords and vision detections. The
interface was migrated from a Next.js web app connected through rosbridge to a native PyQt application that subscribes to ROS~2 topics directly, and it now stores the dialog, logs and detection snapshots of each task for later review.

\subsubsection{Command Interpreter.} This pipeline translates natural language into robot-executable tasks. The process is handled by a fine-tuned Llama 3.1 8B model trained on a synthetically generated dataset. It is locally hosted via the Ollama framework on an Nvidia Orin AGX which provides sufficient VRAM.

The dataset is generated through a structured pipeline that begins with a set of predefined command templates which are populated with random variable entities (e.g., names, locations, and objects) and follow-up instructions. Entries are then generated by outputting a paraphrased natural-language instruction and a corresponding structured action plan composed of predefined primitive actions. During inference, the BAML framework parses the LLM's output into a strict JSON format avoiding action-level hallucinations, and a RAG system grounds commands ensuring entities are present in the current context.

    
This open source research was published by RoBorregos winning 2nd Best Student Paper at the 24th Mexican International Conference on Artificial Intelligence~\cite{10.1007/978-3-032-09037-9_24}.

\subsubsection{Multi-Command Scheduling.}\label{sec:scheduling} The GPSR test awards 200 bonus points for receiving several commands at once, so FRIDA merges them into a single plan instead of executing them one after another. Each action plan produced by the Command Interpreter is split into segments that begin with a navigation action, and a Held-Karp dynamic program~\cite{held1962dynamic} searches for the segment order with the shortest total travel. Two constraints keep every order executable: the actions of each command keep their original order, and, since FRIDA carries one object at a time, a segment from another command may only be interleaved between a pick and its delivery if it does not use the gripper. Travel cost is the length of the path Nav2's global planner would follow, so walls and doors are taken into account; all pairwise paths are obtained in a single batched query before planning, with straight-line distance as a fallback. The merged plan runs as a behavior tree with per-action timeouts and retries; if it fails, the remaining commands are executed sequentially, skipping actions already completed. In a simulated apartment, merging four commands reduced driven distance by 39\% and task time by 26\% compared to sequential execution.


\subsection{Navigation}

\subsubsection{Stack overview.}
FRIDA's navigation is built on Nav2~\cite{macenski2020marathon2}.
SLAM Toolbox~\cite{macenski2021slamtoolbox} builds 2D maps of the arena from
the combined scan of the two RPLIDAR~C1 sensors, using graph-based SLAM with
loop closure, and is then run in localization mode on the saved map. Wheel and
inertial odometry are fused by an EKF on the base controller
and provide the motion estimate for localization.
\subsubsection{Planning and Control.}
Global paths are computed with Nav2's NavFn planner using A* search, while a
Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller~\cite{williams2016mppi}
handles local trajectory tracking. The controller uses a holonomic motion
model, allowing FRIDA to move laterally when approaching furniture or
navigating narrow passages. The local and global costmaps combine the lidar
scan with a spatio-temporal voxel layer built from the RGB-D point cloud,
which captures obstacles outside the lidar plane and lets stale voxels decay
over time, so that phantom obstacles are cleared even while the robot is
stationary.
\subsubsection{Behavior Tree and Supervision.}
Nav2's behavior tree coordinates path planning, goal execution and recovery
behaviors. A custom supervisor monitors the sensor streams and transforms
required by SLAM and Nav2. If any of them becomes unavailable, it pauses both
components and resumes them once the required data is restored. The supervisor
also provides the task manager with a reduced set of navigation services.


\section{Conclusions and future work}

The contributions to the @Home league include general-purpose modules for perception, manipulation, navigation, and HRI, integrated into a cohesive layered architecture. Participation in RoboCup 2026 demonstrated FRIDA’s ability to perform core @Home tasks such as speech-based interaction, person and object recognition, autonomous navigation, and object picking and placing in cluttered environments. Current limitations include two-handed tasks such as lifting a laundry basket or folding clothes, imposed by a single arm with limited reach and an on-arm camera, motivating future work on a bimanual platform, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, imitation learning, and a static camera for real-time feedback.
